Mission Lumen-1: Earth to the Moon

Lumen-1 is sitting in Earth orbit, fueled and ready. Getting it safely to the Moon isn't a straight line — it's six real calculations, back to back. Get one wrong, and the ship doesn't crash and burn — but it does drift, stall, or burn fuel it can't get back. Get all six right, and you land.

Systems Officer: Karis

You're running Mission Control for Lumen-1's run to the Moon. Every leg of the trip hands you a real number problem — how much something weighs once gravity changes, how long a burn takes, how fast the ship needs to go to escape a planet's pull — and the ship only moves when your math is right. Nothing here is decoration: the numbers you calculate are the numbers the mission runs on. Day 2 you'll build your own tabletop model of exactly why gravity bends a ship's path in the first place. Day 3, you brief the room on how the whole mission went.

    Day 2 · Build Day

    Build a Tabletop Gravity Well

    Every calculation you fired yesterday depended on one idea: mass bends the space around it, and that bend is what we call gravity. Today you build a model you can actually see it in.

    Why this build matters

    A planet doesn't reach out and grab a spacecraft. It bends the space near it, and anything moving through that bent space curves — sometimes enough to fall in, sometimes just enough to loop around forever. That looping-around-forever case is exactly what an orbit is. The stretched fabric in this build is a stand-in for that bend, so you can watch it happen instead of just reading about it.

      📖 Glossary

      Mass
      How much matter something is made of — the same everywhere in the universe.
      Weight
      How hard gravity pulls on an object's mass — changes depending on where you are.
      Orbit
      A path where an object keeps "falling" toward a planet but moving forward fast enough to keep missing it.
      Escape velocity
      The minimum speed something needs to break completely free of a planet's gravity.
      Free fall
      Falling with nothing but gravity acting on you — no air resistance, no engine.
      Gravity well
      The dip a massive object creates in the space around it — the deeper the dip, the stronger the pull.
